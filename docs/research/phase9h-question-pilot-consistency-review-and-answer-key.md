**Finding:** no direct contradiction was found in the four records reviewed. Repeated examples descend from the same proposal chain and are not independent source evidence.

For this experiment only, the assumed baseline is:

```text
PARTICIPANT-A + wish + PARTICIPANT-B
```

The intended reading assigns A as perceiver and B as perceived participant. Replacing A with a question word tests a first-slot question; replacing B tests a second-slot question.

- `shuk wish fezo` — candidate for “Who sees the friend?”
- `teshi wish zosh` — candidate for “What does the person see?”

These depend on unverified assumptions: the pilot SVO order is appropriate; `wish` accepts the intended entity frame; and `shuk`/`zosh` can occupy the relevant participant slots. None becomes canon here.

## 3. Four things learners must keep separate

1. **Form:** Does the string follow the proposed pattern?
2. **Role:** Which participant slot is questioned?
3. **Truth:** Is the described event actually happening?
4. **Knowledge:** Does the speaker know the answer?

“Who sees the friend?” asks for the perceiver's identity; it does not assert that anyone actually sees the friend. If a picture does not reveal who is looking at whom, the answer may be unknown even while the exercise's intended slot mapping is clear. Conversely, knowing the scene's answer does not validate the language's grammar.

Unknown answer is not the same as unknown grammar. Missing scene evidence alone neither validates nor disproves a candidate clause pattern.

## 7. Decision and next development step

**Decision:** retain in-situ slot replacement as the single bounded hypothesis for teaching and future testing with `wish`. Do not generalise it to all question words, verbs, registers or complement types. This selects what to test; it does not show that the syntax has passed. No independent learner trial, parser result, historical attestation or canon approval is claimed.

The next substantive grammar task should be **vernacular clause negation**, beginning with meaning and scope rather than importing Formal `GRASH` into Vernacular. The semantic design set should distinguish:

- “The person does not see the friend” — negated seeing event;
- “The person cannot see the friend” — ability or possibility;
- “The person may not see the friend” — permission/possibility, depending on intended reading;
- “No person sees the friend” — quantification, not automatically clause negation;
- “The person does not see who?” — negation and information-question interaction.

These are English semantic fixtures only, not Arcane translations. Before proposing any surface form, check what the sources license and mark new Vernacular forms as proposals until approved.
