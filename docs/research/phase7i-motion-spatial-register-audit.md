# Arcane Phase 7I — motion, spatial relations and register-boundary audit

**Status:** Evidence reconciliation and research proposal; not a new grammar rule  
**Date:** 2026-10-15  
**Canon effect:** none

## 1. Finding

Phase 7H compared ways a vernacular grammar might express source, destination and path. A cross-register audit reveals an important distinction that must remain visible:

- The canonical v3.0-L specification lists formal spatial relations **JO** (toward), **JUR** (away), **JA** (along), **JE** (parallel) and **JI** (aligned/coincident).
- The v5 draft lexicon records these as Formal, CANON, sourced to v3.0-L.
- The same lexicon separately records **desh** (“in/at”), **ding** (“with”), **push** (“for”), **buk** (“about”) and **ges** (“than”) as Vernacular, PROPOSED, sourced to Phase 5.
- The v2.0 role forms **PO** (position role), **FO** (direction role) and **LO** (movement role) are also listed as Formal, CANON in the v5 lexicon.

These entries are not interchangeable merely because they relate to space or roles. The canonical existence of JO/JUR/JA does not establish that those forms are used as everyday prepositions, nor does it prove how a vernacular clause marks a goal or source. Likewise, PO/FO/LO are evidence of older role vocabulary, but their precise argument signatures and productive grammar are not recovered here.

This is a source reconciliation, not a decision to expand or alter any entry.

## 3. Four meanings that must not be collapsed

A reliable motion grammar must distinguish at least these semantic questions:

1. **Location:** where is an entity or event situated?
2. **Goal:** where does a mover end up or move toward?
3. **Source:** where does a mover or transferred theme originate?
4. **Path:** along which route does movement proceed?

Direction/orientation adds another question: toward or away from which reference point? A relation such as “toward” does not by itself establish a destination noun's grammatical role, and a locative “in/at” does not by itself encode movement to a location.

Similarly, “with” may express accompaniment or instrument; “for” may express beneficiary, purpose or intended recipient. The proposed glosses alone do not decide those distinctions.

## 4. Register boundary

The canonical specification explicitly separates Vernacular everyday speech from Formal Arcana. Therefore:

- Do not treat the formal spatial relations as vernacular adpositions without an explicit mapping.
- Do not treat proposed vernacular adpositions as canonical formal operators.
- Do not assume a formal role form PO/FO/LO is a free-standing preposition or a case marker.
- Do not use spelling similarity or English translation to infer identical type, syntax or scope across registers.
- A cross-register translation must preserve the semantic relation, not merely replace one token with another.

A future mapping record should contain: source form; target-register form; relation/type; syntactic position; argument slots; source/version; examples; ambiguity conditions; and whether the mapping is exact, approximate or unavailable.

## 6. Impact on Phase 7H decision

The alternatives remain open. This evidence does not justify selecting fixed order, role particles, adpositions or a hybrid system as canon. It does refine the research task:

1. Recover the original v2.0 definition and examples for PO, FO and LO.
2. Recover source examples for the Phase 5 vernacular adpositions, if any.
3. Specify whether each vernacular adposition is locative, directional, participant-marking, pragmatic or multi-purpose.
4. Define the formal-to-vernacular mapping only after both sides have evidence.
5. Run the motion contrasts and the relevant Phase 7G argument-role tests before approval.
